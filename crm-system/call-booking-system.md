# Call Booking System

The moment a lead shows positive interest is the highest-leverage
moment in the entire pipeline. Most solo founders lose it by moving
too slowly, being too passive, or creating unnecessary friction.

This system removes every point of friction between "interested reply"
and "confirmed call in the calendar."

---

## The Booking Principle

**Never ask "do you want to chat?" Ask "which of these times works?"**

The first is a yes/no decision. The second is a scheduling decision.
Scheduling decisions are easier to say yes to because saying yes doesn't
commit them to anything except a time slot.

---

## When to Introduce the Booking Ask

| Situation | Introduce Booking? | Method |
|---|---|---|
| First positive reply ("sounds interesting") | Yes — but earn it with one narrowing question first | Email or DM |
| After they confirm the pain | Yes — immediately | Email or DM |
| After answering a pricing question | Yes — right after the ROI frame | Email |
| After a "not now" that turned warm | Yes — ask for a specific date | Email |
| On the first cold email (Step 1) | No — ask for a call, not a booking link | Email |
| If they haven't confirmed any pain yet | No — ask a discovery question first | — |

**Rule:** The booking ask comes after they've confirmed a problem exists,
not before. Sending a Calendly link before they see the problem is a
conversion killer.

---

## Booking Script — Email (Primary Channel)

Use this structure immediately after they confirm interest or pain:

```
[Your answer to their question — 2-3 sentences max]

Worth a 10-minute call to see this in action for {{clinic_name}}?

I have [Specific Day, e.g. Tuesday] at [Time, e.g. 2pm EST] and
[Specific Day, e.g. Thursday] at [Time, e.g. 10am EST] open.

Which works better?

[Your name]
```

**Why two specific times?**
- Eliminates the "when are you free?" back-and-forth
- Forces a scheduling decision, not a yes/no decision
- Signals you are organized and respect their time

**Why not Calendly yet?**
Calendly links on cold-to-warm transitions feel automated and transactional.
Use time options for the first call. Send Calendly only after they confirm interest
in a specific slot but need flexibility to pick the exact time.

## Booking Script — DM (Instagram / Facebook)

When reaching out via DM for the first time or following up after a cold email:

**Opening DM (if they haven't replied to email):**
```
Hey {{first_name}} — I sent an email about {{clinic_name}} earlier
this week (subject: [subject line]).

Wanted to make sure it didn't get buried. Quick question:
how do you handle patient calls that come in after your
front desk closes?

Happy to show you what we've built for practices like yours.
Worth a quick look?
```

**Booking DM (after they respond positively):**
```
Great — I can show you the whole thing in 10 minutes.

Are you free Tuesday at 2pm or Thursday at 10am EST?

Just a quick screen share — no pitch, just the demo.
```

**DM rules:**
- Keep every DM under 5 lines
- Never paste a Calendly link in a DM on first contact — reply rate drops sharply
- If they respond positively to DM, move to email for the call confirmation
  (more professional, easier to track in CRM)

## Booking Script — Phone (After Email Attempt)

For HOT leads (score 8+) who haven't replied to 2 emails by Day 5:

```
"Hi, is this [contact name]?

This is [your name] — I sent you a couple of emails this week about
[clinic name] and wanted to make sure they didn't get lost.

I work with dental practices on recovering patient bookings that slip
through when calls go unanswered.

Do you have 2 minutes now, or is there a better time to talk today?"
```

**If they say yes (2 minutes):**
Run a compressed discovery — 3 questions maximum:
1. "How do you handle after-hours calls currently?"
2. "Do you have any sense of how many you miss in a week?"
3. "Would it be worth 10 minutes to see what that number looks like?"

If they answer yes to Q3 → book the call on the phone, right now.
"Great — I have Tuesday at 2pm or Thursday at 10am. Which is better for you?"

**If they say no (not a good time):**
```
"No problem — when's a better time? I can call back today or tomorrow."
```

Do not leave it open-ended. Get a specific callback time before you hang up.

**Voicemail script (20 seconds max):**
```
"Hi {{first_name}}, this is [name]. I sent you a couple of emails about
[clinic name] this week — wanted to make sure they got through.

I'll send you one more email with my number. If you'd rather just call me
back, I'm at [number]. Either way, hope to connect soon."
```

Then send the email immediately:
```
Subject: left you a voicemail — [clinic name]

Hi {{first_name}},

Just left a voicemail — wanted to make sure my emails didn't get buried.

Quick version: I help practices like {{clinic_name}} capture patient
bookings that slip through missed calls. Worth 10 minutes to see if
it fits.

I'm at [number] or just reply here.

[Your name]
```

## The Calendly Introduction (When to Use It)

Introduce your booking link only when:
1. They've confirmed interest in a call
2. The specific times you offered don't work
3. They explicitly ask for a link

**Script:**
```
No problem — here's my calendar so you can grab a time that works:
[Calendly link]

Any slot marked available works for me.

[Your name]
```

Set Calendly to:
- 15-minute slots (not 30 — lower barrier)
- Label the event: "10-min intro call — [Your Name]" (not "Discovery Call" — too corporate)
- Send automated confirmation to you and them
- Include a 1-question intake: "What's one thing you'd want to solve on this call?"
  (This primes them to show up with a specific problem, which makes the call easier)

## Fallback If They Don't Book

### Fallback 1 — They said yes but didn't confirm a time (24h later)

```
Subject: Re: [thread]

Hi {{first_name}},

Just checking — did either of those times work?

If not, here's my calendar: [Calendly link]

Or just tell me what day works and I'll make it fit.

[Your name]
```

### Fallback 2 — They went cold after showing interest (48h later)

```
Subject: Re: [thread]

Hi {{first_name}},

Got quiet — happy to reschedule if [Day] or [Day] didn't work.

One question: is the timing bad in general, or just those specific days?

[Your name]
```

Two options anchors the response: it's either a scheduling problem or
a timing/readiness problem. Their answer tells you which.

### Fallback 3 — Two fallbacks sent with no response

Accept that momentum is gone. Move to the breakup email.
Do not send a third scheduling ask. It signals desperation and costs
you any goodwill you built.

## No-Show Recovery

When they book but don't show up:

**Email sent within 5 minutes of the missed call:**
```
Subject: missed you at [time] — {{clinic_name}}

Hi {{first_name}},

Looks like we got our wires crossed — I was on for [time] but
couldn't reach you.

No problem — still happy to connect. I have [Day] at [time]
or [Day] at [time] available.

Alternatively, here's my calendar if another time is easier:
[Calendly link]

[Your name]
```

**If they don't respond to the no-show recovery within 48h:**
Send one more — shorter:
```
Hi {{first_name}},

Still open to connecting if the timing works out — just reply here.

[Your name]
```

After two no-shows with no reschedule: Status → `Not Interested`. 
Log note: "No-showed twice. Didn't reschedule." Do not chase further.

## CRM Integration for Call Booking

When a call is confirmed, update immediately:

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Status | `Call Scheduled` |
| Meeting Date | The confirmed date and time |
| Next Follow-Up Date | Day before the call (prep reminder) |
| Notes | How you booked it (email / DM / phone), any context they gave |

Set a personal calendar reminder 1 hour before the call:
"Prep: re-read CRM row, check their Google Maps, write 3 questions."

Log the call outcome in Call Log within 5 minutes of hanging up.
Memory decays fast. Log while it's fresh.
