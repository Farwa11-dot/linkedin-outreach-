# Conversation Handling System

Every reply type needs a specific next move. This document is your
conversation decision tree — exact messages, exact logic, no improvising.

---

## The Governing Rule

Every reply you receive has exactly one job to do next:
**move the conversation one step closer to a booked call.**

Not to pitch. Not to send a deck. Not to build rapport over five emails.
One step. Toward a booked call.

If a reply doesn't move toward a call within 2 exchanges, you have lost
conversational momentum and the lead will go cold.

---

## Reply Flow 1 — Vague Interest

### Signals
- "Maybe, let me think about it"
- "Sounds interesting"
- "This could be relevant"
- "I've been thinking about something like this"
- "Not sure if we need it but..."
- "Tell me more"

These replies signal curiosity but no commitment. The wrong move is to
send a wall of information. The right move is one targeted question that
surfaces their specific pain.

---

### Step 1 — The Narrowing Question (Your Reply)

```
Subject: Re: [original thread]

Hi {{first_name}},

Good to hear — quick question so I can make sure this is actually
relevant for {{clinic_name}}:

How do you currently handle calls that come in after your front desk
closes for the day?

[Your name]
```

Short. One question. Requires a specific answer. Their answer tells you
exactly which pain to address next.

---

### Step 2A — If They Describe a Real Gap

They say something like: "We have voicemail but honestly most people don't leave messages."

```
That's exactly the problem. Most patients won't wait for a callback —
they move on to the next result on Google.

I can show you what fixing that looks like in about 10 minutes.
Are you free for a quick call [Day] or [Day] this week?

[Your name]
```

Direct ask. Two specific day options. No Calendly link yet.

---

### Step 2B — If They Deflect or Stay Vague

They say: "We manage okay, I think."

```
Got it. One more question — do you know roughly how many calls
come in outside business hours in a typical week?

Most practices are surprised by the number when they actually look.

[Your name]
```

This surfaces data they probably don't have. Not knowing = a gap.
Their curiosity about that number is your opening.

---

### Step 3 — Close to Call

Once they've expressed or confirmed the gap:

```
Worth a 10-minute look to see if the numbers make sense for
{{clinic_name}}? I can show you exactly what the system catches
and what it would have been worth last month.

[Day] at [time] or [Day] at [time] — which works?

[Your name]
```

**CRM update:** Status → `Replied`, Reply Type → `Positive`, Next Follow-Up Date → tomorrow if no answer.

## Reply Flow 2 — Info Request

### Signals
- "Can you send me some more information?"
- "Do you have a brochure or overview?"
- "Can you tell me more about what you do?"
- "How does it work exactly?"

Info requests are often delay tactics, not genuine asks. Sending a PDF
at this stage loses you the call 80% of the time — they read it alone,
with no one to handle their objections, and they talk themselves out of it.

The correct move: ask what they specifically want to know, then answer
only that — and immediately pivot to a call.

---

### Step 1 — The Specific Ask (Your Reply)

```
Subject: Re: [original thread]

Happy to — what's most useful to know first?

Is it more how the system works, what it costs, or whether
it actually integrates with your setup at {{clinic_name}}?

[Your name]
```

Three options. They will pick one. Now you know what's blocking them.

---

### Step 2A — They Ask "How Does It Work?"

```
Short version: we handle every call that your front desk can't —
after hours, during busy periods, weekends. The caller gets a real
response, gets qualified, and books directly into your calendar.
Your front desk sees everything logged in the morning.

The 10-minute demo shows it better than any description.
Are you free [Day] or [Day] this week?

[Your name]
```

---

### Step 2B — They Ask "What Does It Cost?"

Never answer pricing cold in email. It anchors the wrong number without context.

```
Pricing is based on call volume, so it varies — but for most
practices the size of {{clinic_name}}, it's well under the value
of one recovered patient booking per month.

The quickest way to give you an accurate number is a 10-minute call
where I can understand your actual volume. Does [Day] work?

[Your name]
```

If they push: "Give me a rough range at least."

```
Rough range: [low]-[high]/month depending on setup.
But I'd rather give you a specific number tied to your situation —
a 10-minute call is all it takes.

[Day] at [time]?

[Your name]
```

Give the range only when pushed. Never volunteer it.

---

### Step 2C — They Ask About Integrations

```
Integrates with most calendar and booking systems used by dental
and aesthetic practices — [list 3 most common: Google Calendar,
Calendly, Jane App, etc.].

Takes about 15 minutes to connect during setup. We handle that.

Want to see it working with a quick demo — [Day] or [Day]?

[Your name]
```

---

### Step 3 — If They Go Silent After Getting Info

Send this 48 hours later:

```
Subject: Re: [original thread]

Hi {{first_name}},

Just checking in — did the above answer what you needed?

Happy to answer anything else, or just jump on a quick call if
it's easier than going back and forth.

[Day] or [Day] — does either work?

[Your name]
```

**CRM update:** Reply Type → `Info Request`, Next Follow-Up Date → 2 days.

## Reply Flow 3 — Pricing Question (Direct)

### Signals
- "How much is it?"
- "What's the monthly cost?"
- "Is this expensive?"
- "What does something like this run?"

Pricing questions are actually buying signals — they're evaluating, not browsing.
Don't hide the number. But don't give it without context.

---

### Step 1 — Anchor Before Pricing (Your Reply)

```
Subject: Re: [original thread]

Good question — before I give you a number that means nothing,
let me ask one first:

What's your average new patient value at {{clinic_name}}?
General dentistry, cosmetic, something in between?

[Your name]
```

This forces them to calculate their own ROI before they hear your price.
When they say "$1,200", you can respond with a number that's obviously below it.

---

### Step 2 — After They Give Their Patient Value

```
Okay, so at $1,200 average patient value — recovering even 2 missed
bookings per month means $2,400 in recovered revenue.

Our system runs [price]/month for a practice the size of {{clinic_name}}.

That means you're positive ROI on 1 recovered patient.
Most practices recover 3–6 in the first 60 days.

Worth a 10-minute call to see the math for your specific volume?

[Your name]
```

You have now: given the price, framed it against their number, and
made the ROI case before they can object. Then immediately moved to a call ask.

---

### Step 3 — If They Say "That's More Than I Expected"

```
I get it — [price] sounds like a lot until you run the numbers.

If you recover just 1 patient a month that you would have lost to a
missed call, you're positive. If you recover 3, you're at [3×value]
net positive.

Most of the resistance disappears after the 10-minute demo — because
you see exactly what's being caught, not just what I'm claiming.

Can we do [Day] or [Day] so you can decide with the full picture?

[Your name]
```

---

### Step 4 — If They Say "We Can't Afford It Right Now"

```
Understood — when would be a better time? Q3? After the summer?

I'd rather check back when the timing is right than push something
that doesn't work financially.

What month should I follow up?

[Your name]
```

Get a specific month. Set Next Follow-Up Date. Park it and move on.

**CRM updates:**
- Reply Type → `Positive` (pricing questions are buying signals)
- Status → `Replied`
- Notes → their patient value number (critical for demo later)
- Next Follow-Up Date → 2 days if no call booked

## Conversation Recovery — When You've Lost Momentum

If 4+ days have passed since your last reply and they've gone quiet:

```
Subject: Re: [original thread]

Hi {{first_name}},

Lost me in your inbox?

No pressure — just want to make sure I answered what you needed
before I close this out.

[Your name]
```

Casual. Short. Works because it's unexpected. If no response in 3 days
after this → move to breakup email (see follow-up-intelligence.md).

---

## The 2-Exchange Rule

If a conversation hasn't moved toward a call booking within 2 exchanges:
1. Stop asking open questions
2. Make a direct, specific ask: "Are you free [Day] at [time]?"
3. If they deflect again → park for 30 days, note the objection, move on

Some leads talk because they like the attention. They will not buy.
Recognizing this early saves you weeks of chasing.
