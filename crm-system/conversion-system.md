# Conversion System — Discovery Call, Demo, Close, Objections

Every script here is a starting point, not a word-for-word read.
Know it cold. Then forget it and have a real conversation.

---

## Overview: The Conversion Flow

```
Cold Email Reply
      │
      ▼
[Booking Confirmation] ← send 2 time options, they pick one
      │
      ▼
[Pre-Call Prep] ← 5 min research before you dial
      │
      ▼
Discovery Call (10–15 min)
      │
   ┌──┴──┐
   │     │
Good fit  Not a fit
   │         └── Graceful exit, offer resource, log notes
   ▼
Demo (15–20 min)
      │
      ▼
Soft Close (end of demo or follow-up call)
      │
   ┌──┴──────────────┐
   │                  │
Yes, let's go      Objection → Handle → Re-close
   │
   ▼
Onboarding begins
```

## Pre-Call Prep (5 Minutes Before You Dial)

Open their CRM row. Review:
- Their business name, owner name, industry
- Your personalization note — what gap did you identify?
- Their reply — what exactly did they say? What words did they use?
- Their website and Google listing — anything new since you last checked?

Write down 3 things:
1. The specific problem you think they have (based on research + reply)
2. The question you'll use to confirm that problem
3. The outcome you'll show in the demo if they confirm it

Then put the script away. Use your notes, not the script.

---

## Discovery Call Script (10–15 Minutes)

### Opening (60 seconds)

```
"Hi {{first_name}}, this is [your name]. Thanks for taking the time —
I really appreciate it.

I've got about 15 minutes set aside, and I want to make sure this
is useful for you. My plan is: I'll ask you a few questions to
understand how things work at {{clinic_name}} right now, and
if it looks like there's a genuine fit, I'll show you quickly
what the solution looks like. Sound good?"
```

Wait for their confirmation. This sets collaborative expectations.
You are not pitching. You are diagnosing.

---

### Discovery Questions (5–8 Minutes)

Ask in this order. Do not rush. Let them talk. Take notes in the CRM.

**1. Current situation:**
```
"How do you currently handle calls and inquiries that come in
outside of your regular business hours — evenings, weekends,
when the front desk is tied up?"
```

Wait for the full answer. Do not fill silences immediately.

**2. Quantify the problem:**
```
"Do you have any sense of how many calls you might be missing
in a typical week? Even a rough estimate is helpful."
```

If they don't know (most won't): "That's common — most practices
don't track this. We can actually show you that number."

**3. Probe the impact:**
```
"When a patient calls and no one picks up — what happens?
Do you have a voicemail setup? Does anyone call back?
What's your sense of how many of those callers actually wait?"
```

**4. Understand their patient/client value:**
```
"What does a new patient typically look like for you in terms
of their first visit value? And do you see them long-term
or is it more one-time treatments?"
```

This gives you the numbers for ROI framing later.

**5. Pain point confirmation:**
```
"Based on what you're telling me, it sounds like [restate their
problem in their words]. Is that a fair summary of where the
biggest gap is?"
```

If they say yes → you have permission to present a solution.
If they correct you → update your understanding, restate, confirm.

**6. Decision-making (only if feels natural):**
```
"Out of curiosity — if we found a solution that fit well,
is this the kind of decision you'd make yourself, or does
it involve anyone else?"
```

This surfaces a hidden partner, spouse, office manager, or practice group decision.

---

### Transition to Demo (30 seconds)

```
"Okay — so based on what you've described, I think I can show you
something directly relevant. Let me pull up the demo — it'll take
about 10 minutes and I'll tailor it to what you just told me."
```

---

## Demo Structure (15–20 Minutes)

### Principle: Show, don't tell. And show their problem first.

Open with the gap they described. Close with the solution to that specific gap.
Never demo features they didn't ask about.

---

**Step 1 — Mirror their problem back (2 min)**

```
"So you mentioned {{their exact words about the problem}}.
Let me show you exactly what that looks like from a patient's perspective —
and then what happens when the system is in place."
```

Walk through a "before" scenario:
- Patient calls at 6:45pm
- Phone rings, goes to voicemail
- Patient moves on

Make it concrete. They should feel the missed revenue.

**Step 2 — Show the solution (8–10 min)**

Walk through the live demo or screen-share:
- Incoming call answered immediately (day or night)
- Patient is greeted, asked about their inquiry
- System qualifies the need (new patient? existing? emergency?)
- Appointment booked directly into their calendar
- Confirmation sent to patient via SMS
- Notification sent to front desk for next morning

**What to emphasize:**
- No staff needed after hours
- Works on their existing phone number
- Syncs with their existing calendar system
- Front desk sees everything in the morning — nothing slips

**Step 3 — The ROI moment (3 min)**

```
"Based on what you told me about your patient value — roughly
[their number] — let me show you what recovering just a few
calls per month looks like financially."

Walk through on screen or verbally:
"If you recover 4 missed calls per month, and half of those
book appointments — that's 2 new patients. At [their value],
that's [2 × value] per month.

We're priced well under that. Most clients see ROI in the
first 2–3 weeks."
```

**Step 4 — Check in (1 min)**

```
"Does this solve the problem you described? Is this what
you were picturing, or is there a gap I missed?"
```

Never close before this check. If there's a gap — address it now.

## Closing Script

Use immediately after a positive demo reaction, or at the start of a
dedicated follow-up call if they asked for time to think.

### The Assumptive Soft Close

```
"Based on everything you've told me — the after-hours gap,
the patient value, and what you just saw — does it make sense
to move forward and get this set up for {{clinic_name}}?"
```

Then: silence. Do not fill the pause. The next person to speak loses.

### If They Say Yes:

```
"Great. Here's what happens next: I'll send over a simple
onboarding form — takes about 10 minutes to fill out.
We'll get your phone number and calendar connected,
run a test call, and you'll be live within [timeframe].

I'll send the form to [email] right after this call. Does that work?"
```

Confirm email, thank them, end the call. Send the onboarding form within 10 minutes.

### If They Say "I Need to Think About It":

Do not panic. Do not push. Do ask:

```
"Of course — what specifically would you want to think through?
I want to make sure I've covered everything before we hang up."
```

This surfaces the real objection 80% of the time. Handle it (see objections below).

If they genuinely need time:
```
"No problem. What day works for a 10-minute check-in?
I don't want to leave you in decision limbo — let's put
something on the calendar now so you have a deadline
that's useful for you."
```

Get a specific follow-up call on the calendar before you hang up.

## Objection Handling

### Objection 1: "It's too expensive."

This usually means the ROI wasn't clear enough, not that the price is actually too high.

```
"I hear you. Let me make sure I framed the value correctly —
what would make this feel like an obvious yes from a numbers standpoint?"
```

Or, if they've told you their patient value:
```
"You mentioned your average new patient is worth about $X.
If this system recovers just 2 patients a month — which is
conservative based on what you described — you're at $2X.
We're a fraction of that.

What's making it feel too expensive given those numbers?"
```

### Objection 2: "We already have a voicemail / answering service."

```
"Totally makes sense — can I ask what happens when a patient
reaches that voicemail or answering service?

[Let them answer]

Right — so the question is: of the people who hit voicemail,
how many actually wait for a callback? In our experience,
fewer than 20% do. The rest call the next practice.

What we do is different — the patient gets an actual response
and books in the same interaction, without waiting."
```

### Objection 3: "We're already pretty busy / don't need more patients."

```
"That's a great position to be in. Two follow-up questions:

First — do you have any higher-ticket services you'd like to
do more of, even if volume stays flat?

Second — is there a waiting list situation, or is it more
that your schedule is full but could be fuller with the right cases?"
```

Busy practices still want better patients, higher-value cases, less dependence on
any one referral source. Pivot the conversation to quality over volume.

### Objection 4: "Send me more information."

Almost always means: "I'm not ready to decide but I don't want to say no."

```
"Of course — what specifically would be most useful?
I want to make sure I send something relevant rather than
a general overview that doesn't speak to what we discussed."
```

If they can't specify → the decision isn't close. Ask for a specific follow-up time.

If they specify (pricing, case studies, integrations) → send exactly that,
nothing more. Follow up within 24–48 hours.

### Objection 5: "We're not tech-savvy / our team won't use it."

```
"That's actually a really common concern — and it's why we
set up everything for you. Your team doesn't need to learn
anything new. The system runs in the background.

The only thing that changes for your front desk is that
they see booked appointments already in their calendar
on Monday morning, instead of a list of missed calls to chase."
```

### Objection 6: "I need to discuss it with my partner / practice manager."

```
"Of course — is there a way to get them on a quick call?
Even 15 minutes. I want to make sure they have the full picture
from me directly, rather than you having to relay everything.

When is a time they're both available?"
```

Try to get a three-way call. If not possible, ask what information they need to share,
and send a one-page summary tailored to that person's concerns.

### Objection 7: "What if it doesn't work for us?"

```
"That's fair to ask. Here's how I'd think about it:
We don't do long-term contracts. You're not locked in.

And we run a test before we go live — so you see exactly
what your patients will experience before any real calls
come through.

The real risk is the one you have right now: calls slipping
through with no system to catch them. We're reducing that risk, not adding to it."
```

## Post-Call CRM Update (Do This Within 5 Minutes of Hanging Up)

Open the Leads tab. Update:
- `Status` → appropriate stage (Qualified, Demo Held, Booked, etc.)
- `Notes` → bullet-point summary of what they said, their pain, any numbers they gave
- `Reply Summary` → one-sentence outcome of the call
- `Next Follow-Up Date` → if follow-up needed
- `Meeting Date` → if closed, when onboarding starts

Then send whatever you promised within 10 minutes of the call.
Response speed at this stage signals professionalism.
