# Demo Flow — 10-Minute Discovery + Demo Call

This is the most important 10–15 minutes in your entire sales process.
Every minute is accounted for. Nothing is improvised.

You are not presenting features. You are diagnosing a problem,
confirming it exists at their practice, and showing them exactly what
solving it looks like. In that order.

---

## What You Need Before the Call

### Pre-Call Prep (5 minutes, done the day before)

Open their CRM row. Write these three things on a notepad:

1. **Their specific gap** — what was in your Personalization Note?
   (e.g., "no booking button on Google, closes at 5pm")
2. **Their intake answer** — what did they write when they booked?
3. **One number to find out** — their average new patient value
   (if you don't already have it from the email thread)

Then check their Google Maps listing and website one more time.
Any change since you last looked? New reviews? New hours?

Open tabs:
- Your live demo environment (the actual system running)
- Their Google Maps listing
- A blank notepad for their numbers

---

## The Call Structure

```
0:00–1:30   Opening        Set the agenda. Kill the pitch expectation.
1:30–6:00   Discovery      Diagnose. Listen. Write down their numbers.
6:00–10:00  Demo           Show the fix. Tailored to what they just told you.
10:00–12:00 ROI Moment     Do the math with their numbers live on screen.
12:00–15:00 Close          Ask. Handle one objection. Confirm next step.
```

---

## Minute-by-Minute Script

### 0:00 – 1:30 | Opening

```
"Hi [first name], this is [your name] — thanks for finding the time.

I've got us for about 15 minutes. Here's my plan:

First, I want to understand how things currently work at [clinic name]
around patient calls and inquiries — specifically after hours and
when the front desk is tied up.

Then if it makes sense, I'll show you exactly what we've built to
handle that — takes about 5 minutes to see it live.

And then we'll both know whether it's a fit or not. Sound good?"
```

Wait for their yes. This agenda-setting does three things:
- Signals you're not about to pitch for 15 minutes
- Gets their verbal buy-in before you start
- Frames the call as diagnostic, not transactional

---

### 1:30 – 6:00 | Discovery

**Do not skip this.** Even if you already know their situation from the
email thread, ask anyway. Hearing them say the problem out loud in their
own words primes them to accept your solution as the fix for *their* words.

Ask in this order. Take notes. Never interrupt.

**Question 1 — Current state:**
```
"Walk me through what happens when a patient calls your practice
after your front desk closes for the day. What's the current process?"
```

Wait for the full answer. Common answers:
- "It goes to voicemail" → great, that's your opening
- "We have an answering service" → ask follow-up: "What percentage of those callers actually book?"
- "We're not sure honestly" → perfect, that's the gap

**Question 2 — Volume:**
```
"Roughly how many calls do you think come in outside of business hours
in a typical week? Even a ballpark."
```

If they don't know: "Most practices are surprised when they actually look at the number.
We can figure it out together in a minute."

**Question 3 — Impact:**
```
"Of those calls — what's your sense of how many are new patients
looking to book versus existing patients?"
```

**Question 4 — Patient value (get this number):**
```
"And what's a new patient typically worth to the practice —
first visit and if they become a regular?"
```

Write this number down. You'll use it in the ROI moment.

**Question 5 — Pain confirmation:**
```
"So it sounds like [restate in their words — e.g., 'you're losing a
chunk of after-hours calls to voicemail and most of those people
don't call back']. Is that a fair summary?"
```

If yes: "Okay — let me show you exactly what fixing that looks like."
If no: correct your understanding, restate, confirm.

**What to listen for and write down:**
- Their patient value number (critical)
- Specific time they have the problem (after 5pm? weekends?)
- Who currently handles it (front desk? nobody? answering service?)
- Any past attempt to solve it

---

### 6:00 – 10:00 | Demo

**Screen share on.** Keep your demo environment open and ready.

The demo has three scenes. Move through them in order.

---

**Scene 1 — The Missed Call (30 seconds)**

Show or describe what happens today without the system:
```
"So right now, this is what a patient experiences at [clinic name]
at 6:30pm on a Tuesday:

They search Google, find the practice, call the number —
[simulate: phone ringing] — and it rings to voicemail.
Most of them hang up before the beep. The ones who leave a message?
About 20% get a callback. The other 80% call the next result on Google.

That's the problem we're solving."
```

If you have a real example or data point: use it here.

---

**Scene 2 — The System Live (6 minutes)**

Walk through the live demo in this order:

**Step A — Incoming call answered (1 min)**
```
"Here's what happens with the system in place.

Same patient, same time, same number — except now, the call is
answered immediately. [Show/describe the call being picked up.]

The patient hears a professional greeting specific to [clinic name].
They're asked what they need — new appointment? Existing patient?
Something urgent?"
```

**Step B — Patient qualification (1.5 min)**
```
"The system qualifies them in real time.

New patient? Great — it asks for their name, what they're
coming in for, and their preferred day and time.

[Show this happening in your demo environment.]

Existing patient with an urgent question? It takes a message
and flags it for the front desk first thing in the morning."
```

**Step C — Appointment booked (1.5 min)**
```
"Here's the key part: the patient books directly into your calendar.
[Show the calendar integration — a slot being filled.]

They get an SMS confirmation immediately.

Your front desk? They see a fully booked appointment waiting for them
Monday morning. No missed call to chase. No voicemail to return.
The patient already has a confirmed time."
```

**Step D — Front desk view (1 min)**
```
"And this is what your front desk sees when they open up on Monday.
[Show the dashboard or call log.]

Every call logged. Every booking confirmed. Every missed-call patient
who was previously lost — now in the schedule."
```

**Step E — Tailor to what they told you (1 min)**
```
"You mentioned [their specific pain — e.g., 'you lose a lot of
Saturday morning calls']. Here's how that looks specifically:

[Show or describe Saturday call handling.]

Every call that would have hit voicemail now gets answered and
routed to booking."
```

---

**Scene 3 — Check In (30 seconds)**
```
"Does this solve the problem you described at the start of the call?

Is there a gap I missed — something specific to how [clinic name]
operates that I should address?"
```

Wait for the answer. If there's a specific objection or gap: address it now, not later.

---

### 10:00 – 12:00 | ROI Moment

Take the numbers they gave you and do the math live on screen or verbally.

```
"Okay — you mentioned you're getting maybe 30 calls a week,
and roughly 20% are after-hours.

That's about 6 after-hours calls a week — 24 a month.

You said maybe half of those are new patients looking to book.
That's 12 potential new patients a month hitting voicemail.

At your average patient value of $[their number] —
even if we recover 3 of those 12, that's $[3 × their number]
per month.

Our system runs $[your price]/month for a practice your size.

That means you break even on 1 recovered patient.
Most practices recover 3–6 in the first 60 days.

[Pause. Let them sit with that math.]

Does that math make sense from your end?"
```

Let them react. Do not fill the silence.

---

### 12:00 – 15:00 | Close

**The direct ask:**
```
"Based on everything you've told me and what you just saw —
does it make sense to get this set up for [clinic name]?"
```

**Silence. Do not speak first.**

**If yes:**
```
"Great. Here's what happens next: I'll send you a short onboarding
form — takes about 10 minutes. We'll get your phone number and
calendar connected, run a test, and you'll be live within
[X] business days.

I'll send it to [email] right after this call. Does that work?"
```

**If "I need to think about it":**
```
"Of course — what specifically would you want to think through?

I want to make sure I've addressed everything before we hang up."
```

This surfaces the real objection. Handle it (see conversion-system.md).
Then re-ask: "So does it make sense to move forward?"

**If not interested:**
```
"I appreciate your time — good luck with [clinic name]."
```

Log it. Move on. Do not argue.

---

## After the Call — Next 5 Minutes

1. Update CRM immediately:
   - Status, meeting notes (their exact words on the pain), patient value, outcome
2. If closed: send onboarding form within 10 minutes
3. If "thinking about it": set Next Follow-Up Date = 2 business days
4. Log in Call Log: Action = `Call Held`, Notes = full summary

## Demo Environment Checklist

Before every call, confirm:

```
[ ] Demo environment is loaded and working
[ ] Screen share tested (know which window to share)
[ ] Your calendar is showing real availability
[ ] Their Google Maps listing is open in a tab
[ ] Notepad ready for their numbers
[ ] Phone is silenced
[ ] You are in a quiet space with no interruptions
[ ] CRM row is open for live note-taking
```

---

## Common Demo Mistakes

| Mistake | Fix |
|---|---|
| Starting the demo before hearing their problem | Always discovery first |
| Showing every feature | Show only what solves their specific gap |
| Talking through the ROI before they've seen the demo | Demo first, math after |
| Filling silence after the close ask | Stop talking. Wait. |
| Promising features that don't exist yet | Only demo what is live and working |
| Letting the call run past 20 minutes | Respect their time. Wrap at 15. |
