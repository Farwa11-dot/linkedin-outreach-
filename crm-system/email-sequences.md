# Email Sequences — Missed Calls = Lost Bookings

The core angle: dental and aesthetic clinics miss calls every day.
Each missed call is a patient who doesn't book. That's real, measurable lost revenue.
Your outreach opens with that problem — not with your product.

---

## The Sequence at a Glance

```
Day 0  → Step 1: The Problem (their specific situation)
Day 2  → Step 2: The Proof (a concrete example or question)
Day 5  → Step 3: The Value (one clear thing they gain)
Day 10 → Step 4: The Door (short, no pressure, genuine close)
Day 11 → Archive. Park for 90 days if no reply.
```

---

## Writing Rules Before You Read the Templates

These apply to every email, every step:

**Length:** Under 120 words. Under 100 is better. If you can make your point in 80 words, do it.

**Format:** Plain text only. No bold, no bullet points, no headers inside the email. It should look like something you typed to one person. Because it was.

**One ask per email.** One question. One CTA. Never two.

**Subject lines:** Lowercase or sentence case. No exclamation marks. No spam words. Should look like an email from a colleague, not a marketer.

**Personalization is mandatory.** The `{{personalization_note}}` must be something true and specific. If you cannot fill that in, do not send yet.

## DENTAL CLINICS

---

### Step 1 — The Problem (Day 0)

**Subject options** (test one per week, track which gets more replies):
- `quick question about {{clinic_name}}`
- `missed calls at {{clinic_name}}`
- `{{first_name}} — something I noticed`

---

**Body:**

```
Hi {{first_name}},

I was looking at {{clinic_name}} on Google and noticed 
{{personalization_note}}.

Quick question — how does your practice handle calls that 
come in after hours or when the front desk is busy? 

Most dental practices I talk to lose 3–5 patient bookings 
a week just from missed or unanswered calls. That's usually 
$4–8K in lost revenue every month.

Worth a 15-minute chat to see if that's happening at 
{{clinic_name}}?

{{your_name}}
{{your_title}}
{{your_phone}}
```

---

**Personalization note examples for dental:**
- `you don't have a way to book online through your Google profile`
- `your Google listing shows you're often busy during peak hours`
- `you're closed on Fridays, which is when a lot of patients try to call`
- `there's no after-hours message on your Google Business profile`
- `you have 34 reviews but no responses — patients check that`

---

### Step 2 — The Proof (Day 2)

Send as a **reply to your own Step 1 email** (same thread in Gmail).

**Subject:** *(Leave blank — Gmail will auto-use "Re: [original subject]")*

```
Hi {{first_name}},

Just following up — wanted to share something relevant.

A general dentist in {{city}} was missing about 40 calls 
a month after-hours and on Saturdays. We set up a simple 
system to answer those calls automatically and book patients 
directly into their calendar.

In the first 60 days, they recovered 11 new patient bookings 
they would have otherwise lost.

Not sure if something similar applies to {{clinic_name}}, 
but worth a quick look. Open to a 15-minute call this week?

{{your_name}}
```

---

### Step 3 — The Value (Day 5)

```
Hi {{first_name}},

One more thought before I leave you alone —

The practices that handle this well typically do one thing 
differently: they never let a phone ring to voicemail during 
business hours, and they have something in place for after-hours.

Not a complicated system. Just a reliable one.

If {{clinic_name}} already has this handled, completely 
ignore this. But if it's something you've been meaning to 
look at — I'm happy to show you exactly what's working for 
practices your size.

15 minutes. No pitch. Just a look.

{{your_name}}
```

---

### Step 4 — The Door (Day 10)

```
Hi {{first_name}},

Last one from me — I know your inbox is busy.

If the timing isn't right for {{clinic_name}}, no problem 
at all. I'll check back in a few months.

But if you've ever wanted to know exactly how many calls 
you're missing and what they cost you — I can show you 
that in about 10 minutes, no commitment.

Either way, good luck with the practice. The reviews 
look genuinely strong.

{{your_name}}
```

**Subject options:**
- `{{first_name}} — quick question about {{clinic_name}}`

- `something I noticed about {{clinic_name}}`

```
Hi {{first_name}},

I came across {{clinic_name}} while looking at aesthetic

Quick question: how do you currently handle calls and 
inquiries that come in outside your business hours?

A lot of the clinics I work with are losing 5–10 new client 
inquiries a week just from calls that go unanswered —

treatments.

Would a 15-minute call make sense to see if that's 
something happening at {{clinic_name}}?

{{your_name}}
{{your_title}}
{{your_phone}}
```

---

**Personalization note examples for aesthetic/med spa:**
- `you have strong before/after content on Instagram but no direct booking from your Google profile`
- `your website doesn't show pricing, which means inquirers probably call and often get voicemail`
- `you're running promotions on Instagram but there's no clear way to book them online`
- `your Google Business hours say you close at 5pm — most people research treatments after work`
- `you have 4.8 stars but fewer than 20 reviews — that ratio suggests you're not asking patients to review`

Following up in case this got buried.

they'd recovered over $12K in bookings they would have lost.

Not sure if {{clinic_name}} has this covered already. 
But if it's a gap, it's one of the easier ones to fix.

Open to a quick look this week?

{{your_name}}

## Subject Line Reference

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Name-based | `{{first_name}} — quick question` |
| Clinic-based | `quick question about {{clinic_name}}` |
| Curiosity | `something I noticed about {{clinic_name}}` |
| Problem-based | `missed calls at {{clinic_name}}` |
| Location-based | `{{city}} dental practices — quick question` |

**Never use:**
- Exclamation marks in subjects
- ALL CAPS
- "FREE", "limited time", "guaranteed"
- Deceptive subjects ("Re:" when it's not a reply)
- More than one question mark

## Personalizing the `{{personalization_note}}`

The note goes into the first 2–3 lines and must be:
- **True** — you actually observed this
- **Specific** — not "your clinic looks nice"
- **Relevant** — connects to the missed calls / booking problem
- **Non-threatening** — not "your reviews are bad" → "your reviews have a lot of potential"

**3-minute research process per lead:**
1. Google Maps: check hours, reviews, photos, booking button
2. Website: check if there's an online booking flow, after-hours info, contact page
3. Pick the ONE most relevant gap → that's your note

Do this before you open Gmail. Not during.
