# Offer & Positioning — Dental and Aesthetic Clinics

How to frame what you do, what it's worth, and why it matters to each clinic type.
Every element here should inform how you write emails, run discovery calls, and close.

---

## The Core Problem You Solve

Dental and aesthetic clinics invest heavily in getting patients through the door —
Google Ads, SEO, word of mouth, Instagram. Then a patient calls at 6:30pm.
Nobody answers. No callback system. Patient calls the next clinic on Google.

That booking, and every future booking from that patient, is gone.

**You recover those lost bookings by ensuring every inquiry gets a response,
every time, automatically.**

This is not a marketing pitch. It is a revenue recovery argument.
The money is already being spent to generate the demand. It's leaking out
the back. You seal the leak.

## Dental Clinic Positioning

### The Problem (Their Language)

> "We're fully booked during the day but our phone still rings constantly.
> After 5pm there's no one to answer. On weekends, calls go to voicemail.
> We lose patients we never even knew we had."

### The Stakes

Average new patient value at a general dental practice: **$800–$1,500/year**
Average new patient value at a cosmetic/implant practice: **$3,000–$12,000** (per case)
Average missed calls per week for a busy practice: **8–15**
Percentage of missed-call patients who call the next result on Google: **~67%**

**Back-of-envelope for a general practice:**
- 10 missed calls/week × 4 weeks = 40/month
- 30% would have booked = 12 lost appointments/month
- 12 × $900 average new patient value = **$10,800 lost revenue/month**

**For a cosmetic practice:**
- 8 missed calls/week × 4 = 32/month
- 25% would have booked for a high-ticket procedure = 8 patients
- 8 × $4,000 average case value = **$32,000 lost revenue/month**

These are rough estimates — but they directionally make the point.

### Value Proposition (One Sentence)

> "I help dental practices capture every patient inquiry that currently
> slips through the cracks after hours and on weekends — without adding
> any staff or changing how the front desk works."

### ROI Framing for Dental

Use this structure in emails, calls, and demos:

```
"Most practices I work with recover 3–6 new patient bookings per month
that they were previously losing to missed calls and no-response inquiries.

At [their average new patient value], that's [3 × value] to [6 × value]
per month in recovered revenue.

Our system costs a fraction of that. The math works itself out."
```

Fill in their likely average patient value based on their service mix:
- General: estimate $800–$1,200
- Cosmetic / Invisalign: estimate $3,000–$6,000
- Implants: estimate $4,000–$8,000+

### Proof Points (Use One Per Conversation)

> "A general dentistry practice in Phoenix was losing about 45 calls a month
> to voicemail. After 60 days, they'd recovered 9 new patient bookings.
> That's roughly $9,000 in revenue they would have missed."

> "An orthodontics office in Dallas had no system for after-hours inquiries.
> A parent called on a Sunday about Invisalign for their teenager — no answer.
> They booked elsewhere. We fixed that. In the first month, 4 Sunday
> inquiries converted to consultations."

## Aesthetic Clinic / Med Spa Positioning

### The Problem (Their Language)

> "We run ads. We get DMs and form submissions. But by the time someone
> on our team responds — sometimes the next morning — the client is already
> booked somewhere else. And for treatments like filler or laser,
> they want to book fast when they're excited about it."

### The Stakes

Average consultation value at a med spa: **$150–$400** (consultation)
Average treatment value (first visit): **$400–$1,200**
Average lifetime value of an aesthetic client: **$2,500–$8,000**
Speed-to-response impact: leads contacted within **5 minutes** are 9x more likely to convert

**Back-of-envelope for a med spa:**
- 15 after-hours or weekend inquiries/month (calls, DMs, form submissions)
- 30% would have booked if responded to within 1 hour = 4–5 clients
- 4 × $600 average first treatment = **$2,400/month in recovered revenue**
- Plus lifetime value multiplier = significantly higher

### Value Proposition (One Sentence)

> "I help aesthetic clinics and med spas respond to every client inquiry
> within minutes — day or night — so you capture bookings while the
> client is still excited about the treatment."

### ROI Framing for Aesthetic

```
"Aesthetic clients make booking decisions emotionally and quickly.
If someone sees your Instagram at 9pm, gets excited about lip filler,
and calls you — the window to capture that booking is about 15 minutes
before they've moved on.

The practices I work with that respond within minutes see 40–60% higher
conversion on evening and weekend inquiries.

At [their average treatment value], recovering just 3 bookings a month
more than pays for everything."
```

### Proof Points

> "A med spa in Miami was getting 20+ Instagram DMs per week from people
> asking about botox pricing and availability. Their team was responding
> the next morning. We set up automated first-response within 2 minutes.
> Their DM-to-booking rate doubled in 30 days."

> "An aesthetics clinic in Scottsdale had a weekend problem — Friday evening
> through Sunday, inquiries piled up. By Monday they'd lost most of them.
> They now have a system that answers, qualifies, and books consults automatically.
> Last month, 6 weekend inquiries became Monday appointments."

## Positioning Traps to Avoid

### Trap 1: Leading with features

**Wrong:** "Our AI answers calls and sends SMS follow-ups and integrates with your calendar..."
**Right:** "We make sure you never miss a patient inquiry — including after hours."

Features are proof. Benefits are the pitch. Lead with the outcome.

### Trap 2: Being vague about ROI

**Wrong:** "It can really improve your patient acquisition."
**Right:** "Most practices recover 3–6 new bookings per month. At your new patient value,
that's X to Y per month in recovered revenue."

Specificity creates credibility. Vagueness creates skepticism.

### Trap 3: Comparing to their current system

**Wrong:** "Unlike what you have now, our system..."
**Right:** "How do you currently handle calls that come in after your front desk closes?"

Let them tell you the gap. Then position your solution against the gap they described.

### Trap 4: Overselling before discovery

Do not describe the full product in the first email.
Your first email's job is to get a reply or a call.
The call's job is to understand their problem.
The demo's job is to show the solution to their specific problem.
The close's job is to remove friction from a decision they already want to make.

One job per touchpoint.

## Pricing Conversation Framework

Do not name a price in cold email or in the first minute of a call.
Price anchoring happens after you've established value.

**Step 1:** Quantify their loss (use their numbers, not yours)
> "Based on what you described, it sounds like you might be losing 5–8 bookings a month.
> At your average case value, that's roughly $X per month. Does that feel about right?"

**Step 2:** Anchor against their loss
> "Our system costs significantly less than one recovered booking.
> Most clients see positive ROI within the first 2–3 weeks."

**Step 3:** Give the price as a monthly investment
> "We work on a monthly basis — no long contracts. It's [price]/month.
> That's [price/their avg case value] bookings recovered just to break even."

**Step 4:** Shut up. Let them respond.

## One-Liner Library

Keep these ready for cold emails, calls, and casual conversations:

| Context | One-Liner |
|---|---|
| Opening a cold email | "How does {{clinic_name}} handle calls that come in after the front desk closes?" |
| On a discovery call opening | "Before I tell you anything about what I do — can I ask how you currently handle after-hours patient inquiries?" |
| Responding to "what do you do?" | "I help dental practices stop losing patient bookings to missed calls and voicemail." |
| When asked for pricing early | "Depends on your situation — what's your average new patient value?" |
| Closing the ROI case | "So recovering 4 patients a month at $900 each is $3,600. We're well under that." |
| Handling "we're doing fine" | "That's great — out of curiosity, do you track how many calls you miss per week?" |
