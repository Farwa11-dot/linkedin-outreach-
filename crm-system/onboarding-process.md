# Client Onboarding Process — After They Say Yes

Everything that happens in the first 72 hours determines whether this client
becomes a long-term account or a refund request. Move fast. Be specific.
Make them feel like they made the right call.

---

## The First 10 Minutes After Close

Do these three things before you do anything else:

1. **Send the onboarding email** (template below) — within 10 minutes of hanging up
2. **Update the CRM:** Status → `Closed Won`, Outcome → `Closed`, log the date
3. **Create their row in your client tracker** (separate tab or sheet)

Do not wait until tomorrow. The energy from the call carries forward.
An email that arrives 10 minutes after the call feels like momentum.
One that arrives the next morning feels like a vendor.

---

## The Onboarding Email (Send Within 10 Minutes)

Send from Gmail. Plain text. No attachments yet.

```
Subject: Next steps for [Clinic Name] — let's get you live

Hi [First Name],

Glad we're moving forward. Here's exactly what happens next.

I'm sending you a short setup form — takes about 10 minutes to fill out.
It covers the basics I need to get your system configured:
your clinic's phone number, calendar details, and how you'd like
new patients greeted.

Link: [Google Form URL]

Once I have that, I'll get everything set up and run a test call
so you can hear exactly what your patients will experience before
we go live.

Target: live within [X] business days of your form submission.

If you have questions before then, reply here or call me at [your number].

[Your Name]
```

## The Onboarding Form (Google Forms — Free)

Create one Google Form. Share the link in the onboarding email.
Takes them 10 minutes. Takes you 5 minutes to build.

### How to Build It

1. Go to [forms.google.com](https://forms.google.com) → Blank form
2. Title: `[Your Business Name] — Practice Setup`
3. Description: `Takes about 10 minutes. We use this to configure your system before go-live.`
4. Add these questions in order:

---

**Section 1 — Practice Basics**

```
Q1: Practice name (as you want patients to hear it)
    Type: Short answer | Required

Q2: Primary phone number (the one patients call)
    Type: Short answer | Required

Q3: Practice address (city + state is fine)
    Type: Short answer | Required

Q4: Business hours
    Type: Long answer | Required
    Description: e.g., "Mon–Fri 8am–5pm, closed weekends"

Q5: After-hours hours (if any)
    Type: Short answer | Not required
    Description: Leave blank if fully closed after hours
```

**Section 2 — Greeting & Voice**

```
Q6: How would you like the system to greet callers?
    Type: Long answer | Required
    Description: e.g., "Thank you for calling Bright Smile Dental.
    How can I help you today?" — we'll polish the wording, just give us the idea.

Q7: Is there anything callers should NOT be told or offered?
    Type: Long answer | Not required
    Description: e.g., specific procedures you don't offer, pricing you don't quote over phone

Q8: Any urgent situations the system should escalate immediately?
    Type: Long answer | Not required
    Description: e.g., dental emergencies, severe pain calls
```

**Section 3 — Calendar & Booking**

```
Q9: What calendar system do you use?
    Type: Multiple choice | Required
    Options: Google Calendar / Outlook / Calendly / Other (specify)

Q10: What appointment types should the system offer to new patients?
     Type: Long answer | Required
     Description: e.g., "New patient exam ($150), Whitening consult (free), Implant consult (free)"

Q11: What's your preferred booking window for new patients?
     Type: Multiple choice | Required
     Options: Next available / At least 48 hrs out / At least 1 week out / I'll specify in notes

Q12: Who should receive booking notifications?
     Type: Short answer | Required
     Description: Name + email of the person who manages the schedule
```

**Section 4 — Final Details**

```
Q13: Anything else we should know about how your practice runs that
     would affect how we handle patient calls?
     Type: Long answer | Not required

Q14: Best number to reach you if we have a setup question
     Type: Short answer | Required
```

5. Settings → **Collect email addresses** (so their form submission goes to their email too)
6. After submit message: `Got it — you'll hear from us within 1 business day.`
7. Share → copy the link → paste into their onboarding email

## After the Form Comes In

You'll receive an email notification when they submit. Open it immediately.

### Step 1 — Read the full form (5 minutes)

Check for:
- Any urgent/escalation scenarios you need to handle specially
- Calendar system (if it's not Google Calendar, note what's needed)
- Greeting tone — formal or casual?
- Anything that changes your standard setup

### Step 2 — Reply within 2 hours

```
Subject: Got your form — on it

Hi [First Name],

Got everything I need — thank you.

I'm getting your setup started now. I'll send you a test call
to review before we go live. Expect that within [X] business days.

If anything changes before then, just reply here.

[Your Name]
```

Short. Confirms you received it. Sets timeline expectation.

### Step 3 — Configure the system

Do the actual technical setup. This is specific to your product.
Work from their form answers. Build their greeting. Connect their calendar.
Configure their booking flow.

When done: run a test call from a phone they don't recognize.
Record what the caller hears. Listen back before sending the test to them.

## The Test Call Review

Before going live, they need to approve what their patients will hear.

### Step 1 — Schedule it

Email them:
```
Subject: Your test call is ready — 10 minutes to review?

Hi [First Name],

Your system is configured. Before we go live, I want to walk you through
a test call so you can hear exactly what your patients will experience.

Takes 10 minutes. Would [Day] at [time] or [Day] at [time] work?

[Your Name]
```

### Step 2 — Run the test call review

This is a short call, not a demo. You're reviewing, not selling.

Structure:
1. "I'm going to play you what a new patient hears when they call after hours."
2. Play or walk through the test recording
3. "Does the greeting sound right for your practice?"
4. "Does the booking flow match how you handle new patients?"
5. "Anything you'd change before we go live?"

### Step 3 — Make adjustments if needed

If they want changes: make them same day, send a second brief test clip via email.
Don't schedule another full call unless they request it.

### Step 4 — Go-live confirmation

```
Subject: You're live — [Clinic Name] is now live

Hi [First Name],

You're live as of [time] today.

From this point on, every after-hours call to [phone number] will be
answered and handled by the system.

Over the next 7 days, I'll check in with a quick summary of call activity.
If anything sounds off before then, reply here or call me directly.

[Your Name]
```

## Week 1 Check-In

7 days after go-live, send a check-in. No call needed — just an email.

```
Subject: Week 1 update — [Clinic Name]

Hi [First Name],

One week in. Here's a quick summary:

Calls handled: [X]
New patient bookings captured: [X]
Messages flagged for your team: [X]

Anything that surprised you or that you'd like adjusted?

[Your Name]
```

Populate the numbers from your system's logs. Even if it's early —
showing them real data from week 1 makes the value concrete.

If they reply with a concern: fix it immediately and confirm the fix by email.

## 30-Day Check-In

30 days after go-live, send a second check-in. This one builds the case
for keeping them on (and eventually upgrading).

```
Subject: 30 days in — how [Clinic Name] is doing

Hi [First Name],

One month in. Here's what the system has handled for your practice:

Total calls: [X]
After-hours calls answered: [X]
New patient bookings captured: [X]
Estimated revenue recovered: [X bookings × $Y avg patient value = $Z]

Compared to month 1 in similar practices, you're [above/at/below] average
on recovered bookings. [Add one specific observation.]

Everything still running smoothly on your end?

[Your Name]
```

Framing the value in dollar terms (bookings × their patient value) is the
most important thing you can do in this email. It makes renewal a no-brainer.

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| Day 0 (close) | Send onboarding email within 10 min. Update CRM. |
| Day 0–1 | Form submitted. Acknowledge within 2 hours. |
| Day 1–3 | Configure system. Run internal test call. |
| Day 3–5 | Test call review with client. Adjustments if needed. |

The goal: no surprises. They always know what's happening next.
Every step confirms they made the right decision.

## What to Do If Something Goes Wrong

### System isn't working correctly

1. Fix it before telling them — if you can fix it in under 2 hours
2. If it'll take longer: email them proactively before they notice
3. Never go silent when something breaks

Email template:
```
Hi [First Name],

I noticed [specific issue] in your system today. I'm fixing it now —
should be resolved within [timeframe].

No calls have been missed. I'll confirm when it's corrected.

[Your Name]
```

### They're unhappy with something

Reply within 1 hour. Get on a call within 24 hours.
Don't argue. Identify the specific complaint. Fix or explain.
Follow up after fixing: "Is that working the way you expected now?"

### They want to cancel

Don't fight it. Ask one question:
```
"Before we close it out — is there one specific thing that didn't
work the way you expected? Even if you've decided, it helps me
improve the system for other practices."
```

This surfaces real feedback and occasionally re-opens the conversation.
If they've decided: process the cancellation gracefully. Leave the door open.
